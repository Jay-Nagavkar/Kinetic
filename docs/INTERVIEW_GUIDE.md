# KhelSetu: Field Interview Guides & Institutional Instruments

---

## 1. Student Field Interview Protocol (15–20 Hostel Students)

* **Goal**: Understand daily movement routines, dorm room barriers, and reaction to Silhouette Mode & fair squad scoring.
* **Duration**: ~20 minutes per participant.

### Questions
1. **Current Physical Routine**:
   - In a typical college week, how many days do you do any intentional exercise (walking, gym, sports, room workouts)?
   - What happens to your physical activity when mid-semester or final exams start?
2. **Dorm Room Friction**:
   - Have you ever tried working out in your hostel room? What felt awkward? (Lack of space, roommates watching, embarrassment).
   - If a fitness app uses your camera to count reps, how do you feel? Does showing *only an animated neon stick-figure* (Silhouette Mode) make you feel comfortable?
3. **Squad Scoring Dynamics**:
   - In previous group fitness challenges, have you felt discouraged if someone was way fitter than you?
   - If your squad score was based on *how much of your personal target you achieved* (e.g. 80%) rather than raw reps, would you feel more motivated to participate?

## 2. Sports Officer / Physical Education Director Interview Guide (2–3 Officers)

* **Goal**: Validate institutional reporting needs, facility planning workflows, and $k$-anonymity dashboard utility.
* **Duration**: ~25 minutes per officer.

### Questions
1. **Institutional Visibility**:
   - How does the college currently track whether general undergraduate students (non-athletes) are maintaining baseline physical fitness?
   - What reports are you required to submit annually for Fit India, university accreditation (NAAC), or sports funding?
2. **Observatory Utility**:
   - If an anonymized dashboard shows you weekly active minutes per hostel or department, what specific decisions would that help you make? (e.g. allocating floodlight hours, scheduling intramural tournaments, purchasing gym equipment).
3. **Data Privacy Guardrails**:
   - Our system conceals any cohort with fewer than 10 students ($k \ge 10$) and displays only aggregate adherence percentages. Does this satisfy your institutional ethics and privacy requirements?
